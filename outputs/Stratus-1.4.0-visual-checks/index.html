What changed

Snell-law refraction at water IOR 1.333, dielectric Fresnel and underwater total internal reflection. A shared cloud environment supports reflected and transmitted sky, while clipped screen-space rays resolve visible terrain and entities. Biome tint, sediment, rain runoff, local skylight, cave shelter and lamps influence water. Flowing sheets use bounded thickness, avoiding deep-lake absorption against the sky.

Moisture favours visible concave corners and upward porous faces; walls drain. No random dampness noise. Defaults: vanilla shading mix 1.00, water clarity 1.70, saturation 1.00. Cloud integration covers the full intersected volume with a continuous horizon limit. Actual vanilla fancy clouds and textured sun/moon are independent options.

Scientific basis: PBRT: Snell and Fresnel, USGS: turbidity and runoff, Lagarde: wet surfaces. Biome sediment rules are visual heuristics, not measurements or a fluid simulation.

Scope and remaining approximations

Screen-space optics cannot reconstruct hidden/off-screen geometry. The directional sky cache uses the camera origin, so nearby low-cloud parallax is approximate. Vanilla sun textures and cloud meshes render in the view, but their exact off-screen shapes are not reconstructed by the fallback cache. Refraction uses one opaque depth layer and bounds displacement to available screen coverage; flowing-sheet thickness and cavity retention are geometric approximations. No displaced waves, full fluid simulation or cast shadow maps. Finite tests cannot cover every world and resource/mod combination.
