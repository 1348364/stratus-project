Water and wet materials

Perspective-correct Snell rays use actual opaque scene depth and preserved receiver normals. The wooden test structure exposes corners, steps, thin gaps and different depths. Physical distortion is1.00; stronger settings are deliberately artistic. Fresnel, underwater total internal reflection and shared water extinction apply above and below the surface.

Wet darkening now follows material absorption. Soil and raw timber darken more; polished stone less; metal, glass and glazed ceramics have no pore darkening. Surface-water sheen covers whole exposed faces independently, with no corner-retention or random dampness mask. These block classes are visual heuristics.

Clouds, fog and controls

An original128³ weather texture drives rounded cloud body, finer erosion and spatial fog pockets. Cloud lighting samples sun transmission and ambient occlusion; fog integrates extinction and directional light along the view ray. All previous controls remain, with seven new controls, useful wider ranges and a separate refraction submenu. Vanilla shading mix1.00, water clarity1.70 and saturation1.00 remain the defaults.

Quality takes priority. High uses full-resolution reflection/atmosphere buffers, a512×256 sky cache and up to256 refraction samples. The previous60fps goal was withdrawn; this release makes no frame-rate promise.

Visual observations

  • The submerged wooden fixture changes apparent depth and block placement relative to its unbent control. Logs, steps, slabs and fence gaps were checked from several angles; the underwater surface view shows the Snell window and total internal reflection.
  • Colored columns and the cow appear in the lake reflection; the reflection-disabled control removes ordinary scenery reflections. Deep/shallow, thin falling water, dark/lit caves, shore corners, rain/night and elevated water over lower clouds were inspected.
  • Swamp water is visibly muddier than warm-ocean water; river and frozen-ocean variants remain distinct without ice contamination. Expanded clarity and artistic-distortion controls produce visible changes.
  • Absorption-only material views darken soil, timber and raw stone more than polished stone; iron and glazed ceramic retain their dry substrate. Broad film remains independent of darkening, including at grazing angles. Stone and ore use the same substrate class.
  • Cloud views show a continuous filled volume from below, inside and above, with rounded relief and a separate upper layer. Default fog leaves nearby trees clearer while introducing spatial pockets between deeper tree layers; stronger controls move the fog closer/higher.
  • Custom sunlight remains warm and locatable through overcast; vanilla cloud/sun/moon options render their actual geometry/textures. Nether/End, entity form lighting, breaking particles, the settled snowball/egg/pearl repeat, fireflies/bloom and thirteen option screens were inspected.
  • Some newly revealed faces, thin fence/glass layers and screen edges can still show missing or blended refraction detail because only one visible opaque depth layer is available. This remains an approximation, not a claim of artifact-free path tracing.

Limits and test scope

  • Screen-space rays cannot recover hidden or off-screen geometry, or independent transparent depth layers. Disocclusions and screen-edge fallback transitions can remain.
  • Material absorption classes are artist-tuned block heuristics, not measured porosity or PBR texture maps.
  • Cloud and fog scattering, weather and biome sediment are approximations. Sky-cache origin introduces cloud parallax approximation.
  • Shading waves do not displace block geometry. No cast terrain shadow maps or full fluid simulation.
  • Visual inspection is finite; heavily modified resource packs and every world configuration are not covered.

The isolated client is left open at the submerged wooden structure. Main's existing shader selection and non-shader files are preserved. The coverage includes common water scenarios, three dimensions and thirteen settings views, rather than every possible world.
